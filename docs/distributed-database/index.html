Replication & Distributed Data — Solution Architect Roadmap (Part 1)

How to use this: each phase has a Goal, Concepts to master, and a Todo checklist. Do the phases in order — later phases assume the earlier ones are solid. Treat every unchecked box as your backlog. Part 2: Replication Mechanism — The 4 Dimensions.

0 / 52 todos checked, across 10 phases  · 

Phase 0 — Prerequisites (Don't Skip)

Goal: Have the vocabulary before the deep dives make sense.

Concepts: CAP theorem, ACID vs BASE, distributed systems basics (clocks, network partitions), what a WAL/binlog is, leader/follower terminology.

  • → masterclass
  • → deep dive

Phase 1 — Core Replication Theory

Goal: Understand replication as a mechanism, independent of any specific database.

Concepts: Single-leader (master–slave), multi-leader (master–master), leaderless (Dynamo-style), synchronous vs asynchronous vs semi-synchronous, statement-based vs row-based vs logical vs physical replication.

  • → deep dive

Phase 2 — Consistency Models (The Architect-Level Filter)

Goal: Be able to pick the right consistency model for a business requirement, not just the strongest one.

Concepts: Strong consistency, eventual consistency, read-your-writes, monotonic reads, causal consistency, linearizability, quorum reads/writes (N, R, W).

Goal: Know how the theory maps to the tools you'll actually specify in an architecture doc.

Phase 4 — Failover, Split-Brain, and Consensus

Goal: Understand what happens when things go wrong — this is where architects earn their title.

Concepts: Automatic vs manual failover, split-brain, fencing, quorum-based leader election, Raft/Paxos at a conceptual level, STONITH.

Phase 5 — Geo-Distribution & Multi-Region Replication

Goal: This is where most "advanced" interviews and real design reviews actually live.

Concepts: Cross-region replication latency, active-active vs active-passive, conflict resolution (LWW, CRDTs, application-level merge), data locality/residency (GDPR-type constraints), read replicas for latency reduction.

Phase 6 — Monitoring, Observability & Debugging Replication in Production

Goal: Be the person who can diagnose a replication incident at 2am, not just design the diagram.

    • Replica lag spiking under normal load
    • Replica falls out of sync entirely / needs re-seeding
    • Primary disk filling up because a replica is disconnected (WAL/binlog retention)
    • Read-after-write inconsistency reported by users

Phase 7 — Security & Compliance in Replicated Systems

Goal: Don't let replication become the compliance hole in your architecture.

Goal: The actual solution-architect skill — combining everything into a defensible design.
